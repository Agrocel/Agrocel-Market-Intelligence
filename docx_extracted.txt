1. Executive Summary
Agrocel's people are in the market every day — meeting clients, negotiating with suppliers, attending exhibitions, and speaking with advisors and co-players across geographies. Today, the intelligence gathered through these interactions lives in scattered WhatsApp chats, inboxes, visit reports, and individual memory. It is rarely consolidated, rarely graded for reliability, and often lost. We started using CRM however, not utilizing and capitalizing enough due to ease of use and its own limitations.
This blueprint proposes a single, AI-interfaced Market Intelligence (MI) Platform where every relevant team — Sales & Marketing, Business Development, Procurement, and Global Advisors (Israel, China, and others) etc. continuously feeds market signals in whatever form is natural to them: a WhatsApp text or voice note, a forwarded email, a meeting or visit report, or notes from a call or conference. The AI layer converts these raw inputs into structured, searchable intelligence, tags them to our products, segments, clients, suppliers, and competitors, grades them by reliability (trusted intel vs. rumour).
In parallel, the platform will automatically monitor approved external sources — industry and competitor websites, Stock exchange updates for annual reports, financials etc., import/export data, and our existing paid subscriptions — so that human intelligence and market data reinforce each other in one place and presents them on role-based dashboards for decision-makers
The purpose of this document is to secure leadership alignment on the framework, scope, and governance before detailed design and platform development begin.
2. Vision & Objectives
Vision: To make market intelligence a continuous, collective, and compounding asset of Agrocel — captured once, structured by AI, and available to every decision-maker who needs it, when they need it.
Objectives
Capture every valuable signal from the field before it is lost — with near-zero extra effort for the contributor.
Convert unstructured inputs (voice, chat, email, reports) into structured, tagged, searchable intelligence using AI.
Separate signal from noise by grading each item on source reliability and information credibility (trusted vs. rumour).
Give leadership and teams live dashboards of what is happening across products, segments, clients, suppliers, and competitors.
Blend human intel with automated monitoring of external sources (websites, import/export data, subscriptions).
Protect sensitive commercial information through role-based permissions for feeding, viewing, and editing.

5. The AI Interface — How Raw Input Becomes Intelligence
Every input, regardless of channel, passes through the same AI pipeline:
Step
What the AI does
1. Ingest
Receives the WhatsApp message, voice note, email, report, or web content
2. Convert
Transcribes voice notes; extracts text from emails, attachments, and documents; translates where needed
3. Structure
Identifies the core intel: what happened, who is involved, which product/market, price/quantity figures, dates
4. Tag
Auto-tags to Agrocel's taxonomy: product, segment, industry, client, supplier, competitor, geography
5. Grade
Proposes a reliability grade (Section 6); contributor/curator can confirm or adjust
6. Link
Connects related items (e.g., three independent reports of the same intel strengthen each other)
7. Present
Feeds dashboards, alerts, and periodic digests; answers questions from users
Users can also query the platform conversationally — e.g., “What have we heard about competitor strategy or expansion plans in the last 90 days?” — and receive an answer compiled from graded intel with sources.
6. Intelligence Classification — Trusted Intel vs. Rumour
Every item carries a reliability grade, so users always know the weight to place on it. Grades combine the source's track record with how well the information is corroborated:
Grade
Label
Meaning
A
Trusted / Confirmed
From a reliable source and corroborated (website document, multiple independent reports, or direct first-hand knowledge)
B
Reliable
From a source with a good track record; not yet independently corroborated
C
Unverified
Plausible but single-source; needs corroboration before acting
D
Rumour
Market talk / hearsay — logged because rumours matter, but clearly flagged as such
The AI proposes a grade; designated curators can upgrade/downgrade based on judgement.
Grades are dynamic, a rumour corroborated by two later reports is automatically flagged for upgrade.
Dashboards weight and visually distinguish intel by grade, so rumours never masquerade as facts.
7. Dashboards & Outputs
For CEOs / JMD — Leadership dashboard — cross-business view: key developments, emerging risks and opportunities, competitor moves, high-grade intel of the week.
For teams — Product & market views — intel filtered by product, segment, client, supplier, competitor, or geography.
Real-time — Alerts — immediate notification when high-impact intel arrives (e.g., a major client issue, a competitor price action, a supply disruption).
Periodic — Digests — AI-compiled weekly/monthly intelligence summaries per business, emailed automatically.
On demand — Ask-the-platform — natural-language search across the full intel base with cited sources.

9. Governance & Confidentiality
A designated Market Intelligence (MI) owner per business drives adoption, curation quality, and source-list expansion.
Clear ground rules on what may be captured, respecting confidentiality obligations to clients and suppliers and applicable data-protection norms.
External web monitoring of approved list of legitimate, public, or duly-subscribed sources.
Periodic review (quarterly) of intel quality, contribution levels, and dashboard usefulness by leadership. Present the designed part of Dashboard to Chintan Shivir & IBM level meetings.

11. How We Will Measure Success
Contribution: number of intel items fed per week, per team, and per channel.
Coverage: share of key products, clients, suppliers, and competitors with fresh intel.
Quality: proportion of Grade A/B intel; rumours later confirmed or refuted.
Usage: dashboard views, queries asked, digest readership by decision-makers.
Impact: documented decisions (pricing, sourcing, client actions) influenced by platform intel.
